Moving parts and keyframes

Many stages have parts that move: platforms that slide, bridges that swing, floors that ripple. The keyframe editor lets you change how they move. Open it with Stage > Animation keyframes (Ctrl+K) or the Keyframes… button in the 3D view. It opens below the views and switches to the 3D view so you can watch the result.

Parts and channels

The list on the left shows each moving part with six channels: Move X, Move Y and Move Z (sideways, up and down, forwards and back) and Turn X, Turn Y and Turn Z (tilting, turning and rolling). The second column says whether a channel already moves in the original stage, stays fixed, or has your keys. Hover over a part to see whether its collision moves with it and how many objects ride on it.

The curve

The middle of the panel draws the selected channel over time. The grey dashed line is the original motion, the blue line is the motion that will be saved, and the diamonds are your keys. The red line is the current frame: click or drag anywhere on the curve to move it, and the 3D view follows. The game runs at 60 frames a second, so frame 60 is one second in.

Editing with keys

Pick a channel and press Edit with keys. The editor turns the channel's current motion into a handful of keys that reproduce it closely, so you start from what's already there. Then drag a key left or right to change when it happens and up or down to change the value. Double-click the curve to add a key, or press Add key to add one at the current frame. Del deletes the selected key, and the arrow keys nudge it by one frame or one step. If you'd rather type, the selected key's frame and value are under Selected key. Hold Shift while dragging for fine steps.

Curve types

Each key has a curve that describes how the value travels to the next key. Linear moves at a steady speed. Ease In starts slowly and speeds up, Ease Out slows down as it arrives, and Ease In/Out is gentle at both ends. Bézier lets you shape the timing yourself with two orange handles; pull a handle above the top or below the bottom and the motion overshoots. Smooth (spline) draws a smooth path through the key and its neighbours, and Hold stays put until the next key and then jumps. Change it with the Curve to next box, or right-click a key.

Before the first key the part stays at the first key's value, and after the last key it stays at the last one. If a channel ends somewhere other than where it started, the panel warns you that the part will jump when the animation starts over.

Length

Animation length changes how long one loop takes, in frames. Everything is stretched or squeezed to fit, keys included, so fewer frames make the whole stage faster and more frames make it slower. Original puts the stage's own length back.

Original motion removes a channel's keys. The panel also shows how big the animation has become; if it grows bigger than anything that has been tried in the game, it says so.

Only the stage's own moving parts can be animated. Platforms you add are part of the fixed stage, and a new stage made with Create New Stage has no moving parts.
