The 3D view

Press Ctrl+2 (or click the 3D view tab) to see the stage with its own textures, your objects and your platforms. If two small extras the 3D view needs (PyOpenGL and numpy) are missing, the 3D view offers to install them; everything else works without them.

Moving around

Drag with the left mouse button to turn the view, drag with the right button to pan, and use the wheel to zoom. The buttons above the view jump to useful angles: Start view (Home) puts you behind the start, Whole stage (F) fits everything in, and From above (T) looks straight down.

Editing in 3D

Click an object or a platform to select it, then drag it to move it. It stays at its own height while you drag, so you can line things up by eye and fine-tune the numbers on the right afterwards.

Watching the stage move

If the stage has moving parts, Play sets them in motion and the slider next to it scrubs through the animation frame by frame. Collision (C) moves along with its part, so you can see exactly where the ball will be able to roll at any moment. The Keyframes… button opens the keyframe editor.
