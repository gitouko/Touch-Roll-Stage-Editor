Platforms and collision

The Geometry menu adds simple shapes to a stage. Pick one, then drag a rectangle on the top view to set its size.

Add platform (P) makes a box that's drawn and solid, which is what you'll want most of the time. Give it a thickness of 0 and it becomes a flat panel. Add invisible floor (I) makes something solid that isn't drawn, which is handy for safety nets or for hidden shortcuts. Add decoration is the opposite: it's drawn, but the ball goes straight through it. Remove collision in an area (R) makes the ball fall through whatever the stage has there, while the drawing stays as it is. Tick Only within a height range to limit it to one floor of a stage with several levels.

New platforms are drawn with one of the stage's own textures, so they fit in with the rest of the stage. They always belong to the fixed part of the stage.

Changing a shape

Drag a shape on the top view to move it, or on the side view to change its height. On the right you can type its exact extent (X from / to, Z from / to), the height of its top and its thickness, and choose whether it's drawn, solid or both.

Size does the same job in one row: X is the width, Y the thickness and Z the depth. Changing them keeps the platform centred where it is, and its top stays at the same height. Tick Keep proportions to scale all three together.

Textures

The Texture list shows the textures the stage itself uses, with a small picture of each, and the stage's main floor texture first. Pick one to draw the platform with it. Every stage has two to five of them: floor grids, edge colours and so on. Textures only used by moving parts can't be offered, because anything drawn with them would move too. Platforms lay their texture on from above, so an edge texture shows as stripes on a platform's top; that's expected. Your own pictures as textures are planned for version 1.1.

Turned platforms

A platform can be turned on all three axes: Tilt, Swivel and Rotate. With them you can build ramps, diagonal paths and banked curves. Tilt leans it about its own x axis, Swivel turns it on the spot like a door, and Rotate rolls it about its own z axis (Rotate makes a ramp that rises along x). Q and E swivel the selected platform 15° at a time. A platform turns about its own middle, and the ball rolls up and down turned platforms just as it does on the game's own slopes.

Things to keep in mind

You can build past the edge of the original stage: the editor grows the stage's collision area to fit. How far out a platform can be drawn depends on the stage. If you go too far, the editor says so and points you to Stage > Drawing range.

Shapes are applied in the order you added them, so a no-collision area only removes collision that was there before it, not platforms you add later.
